Passphrase shards (optional but recommended)

Split the passphrase into Shamir shards so k-of-n beneficiaries can reconstruct it together if you die without telling anyone. Each beneficiary can read only their own shard. You still keep the passphrase yourself; this is a safety net, not a replacement.

Encrypted instructions (optional)

How it works

  1. 1The owner lists beneficiaries and shares (must total 100%), sets the check-in interval and grace window, and writes a passphrase.
  2. 2The browser encrypts the instructions with the passphrase. The contract stores the ciphertext and the keccak256 hash of the normalized passphrase.
  3. 3Every beneficiary gets a soulbound claim ticket, minted to their wallet at setup. Transfers revert.
  4. 4The owner checks in on schedule. If the deadline plus grace passes, beneficiaries can claim their percentage of the escrow.
  5. 5A beneficiary submits the passphrase to claim. Then they read the instructions, decrypted with that passphrase.
Important: the passphrase is sent in the claim transaction, so it becomes public on-chain at claim time. The instructions are ciphertext on-chain, but after a claim anyone who reads the chain can try to decrypt them with that passphrase. Do not put anything in the instructions you would not want public after a claim. The first claim freezes the escrow snapshot.

Built on LastKey: two claim modes, cross-chain claims, webhooks, keepers

  • STwo claim modes. The owner picks a mode per vault. Specific assets (listed): the beneficiary sweeps only the ERC-20 tokens the owner listed, within the caps. Everything: the owner records a blanket-approval attestation on-chain and approves the vault for their tokens. The keeper then scans the owner wallet on this chain, checks each token's allowance, and sweeps what is approved and held, plus escrowed native balance, in batches. The contract never enumerates your wallet and never takes your key. Full-wallet recovery through a smart account is v4 roadmap, not live.
  • XCross-chain claims. A beneficiary can claim a mirrored share of the escrow on a second EVM chain. Watchers sign each claim, and the destination contract pays out only once a quorum signs. The design is an interface, so other bridges can plug in. On the Portaldot testnet the watcher signers run locally.
  • NLastKey-Notify webhooks. Vaults emit structured events: check-ins, funding, a timer warning at 80% of the interval, and claim opened. Apps can subscribe with a vault id and get signed HTTP callbacks, so a family dashboard can show "still active" without reading the chain. The service is self-hostable.
  • KKeeper automation. A small script watches vaults and can claim for a beneficiary once the window opens, using their own funded key and their passphrase. Anyone can run a keeper. Keeper fees are roadmap, not live.
Proof of life still counts wallet transactions, not only the check-in button. Webhooks and keepers never hold the passphrase unless you give it to them.
